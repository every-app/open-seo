Google decides whether a service business shows up in the map pack by asking three questions of each search: do you do the thing, are you near the person searching, and do people vouch for you. You control two of them. This guide runs all three on a real remodeler in Cookeville, Tennessee, and gives you the prompts to run the same checks on your own business in OpenSEO.

## How Google picks the map pack: three questions, and you control two

The map pack is the block of three businesses with a map that sits above the regular results when someone searches for a local service. Google's own [local ranking page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) names the factors it uses to fill it: relevance, distance and prominence. For a business that sends a truck, they break down like this.

- Relevance is "how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for." That's your categories, your services list and your description. You control all of it, today, for free.
- Distance is how far you are from the person searching. You can't move the shop. You can stop measuring your rankings from inside it, which is the mistake most owners make.
- Prominence is how well-known you are. Google puts it this way: "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." You control this too, over months.

Knowing the three factors won't tell you which one is costing you jobs. To find that, you have to check each one against a real business, and that's what the next five sections do with a remodeler that has perfect reviews.

And you're not the only one who's noticed there's money in the trades. [Benjamin Behrooz](https://unscriptedseo.com/benjamin-behrooz-empathy-blue-collar-marketing-ai/), who runs street-team marketing for home service companies out of Los Angeles, put it this way on the podcast:

![A quote card reading: We know the future millionaires of this country are gonna be blue collar workers and right now other people know this too. Attributed to Benjamin Behrooz, Branding Los Angeles, from The Unscripted SEO Interview Podcast.](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses/quote-future-millionaires.png)

Other people knowing it means a crowded map, and on a crowded map the settings you control carry more weight.

## Local keyword research: find the words customers use to hire you

Before you touch your profile, find out what people type when they want to hire you. Your vocabulary and theirs often don't match. We ran H&H's main service, `kitchen remodeling`, through OpenSEO's [keyword research](/docs/skills/keyword-research), and this is the raw result:

![OpenSEO Keyword Research results for the seed kitchen remodeling in the United States, September 28, 2026, dark theme. 149 keywords. Top rows: Kitchen Cabinets 246,000, High End Kitchen 201,000, Craft Street Kitchen 110,000, Kitchen Remodeling Near Me 74,000 with a difficulty score of 15, Kitchen Remodel 60,500, Preppy Kitchen 49,500.](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses/keyword-research-kitchen-remodeling-openseo.png)

Look at what sits above the one you want. `kitchen cabinets` at 246,000 a month is people shopping for boxes. `preppy kitchen` is a cooking channel. `kitchen island ideas` is somebody on a Sunday afternoon with a Pinterest board. Those searchers aren't hiring a contractor. Ranking for them would put you in front of [the wrong half of your audience](/blogs/wrong-half-of-your-audience).

The search that pays is fourth: `kitchen remodeling near me`, 74,000 a month, difficulty 15. Right behind it is `kitchen remodel` at 60,500 and `kitchen renovation` at 18,100. Those three are your customer talking. The rest of the list is noise you'll be tempted to chase because the number is bigger.

Those are national numbers, so use them to learn _which words_ people use. They don't tell you how many of those people live near you. OpenSEO can pull volume for a single city or county if you give it one, at an extra credit cost per seed. For a town the size of Cookeville, expect small numbers, and don't let that scare you off. [Jonathan Schüßler](https://unscriptedseo.com/how-one-freelancer-ranks-in-50-german-towns-with-programmatic-seo/), who does SEO for service businesses in Germany, built a wedding photography business on search terms other photographers ignored for being too small:

![A quote card reading: Why not target the one where all the most expensive stuff is happening, but there's not many clicks. Attributed to Jonathan Schüßler, freelance SEO for service businesses, from The Unscripted SEO Interview Podcast.](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses/quote-not-many-clicks.png)

For a remodeler, "where the expensive stuff is happening" is the kitchen and the addition. The drywall patch can wait. Pick the two or three services that carry your margin and do this step for each. The [keyword research library](/library/keyword-research) goes deeper on seeds and filtering, and the [keyword clustering skill](/docs/skills/keyword-clustering) will group what's left by service.

```text
Use OpenSEO to research keywords for my business. I'm a [trade] in [town, state].
Seeds: "[main service]", "[second service]", "[main service] near me".
Keep only searches from someone who wants to hire a business like mine.
Drop product shopping, DIY ideas, brand names, jobs and anything about a
different trade. Group what's left by the service it belongs to, and give
me volume and difficulty for each group, highest-value service first.
```

## Google Business Profile optimization starts with your categories

Now pull your own Google Business Profile (called Google My Business until 2021), and read it like a stranger would. OpenSEO's [Local SEO skill](/docs/skills/local-seo) starts here too. This is H&H's profile, re-checked in OpenSEO on September 28, 2026:

| Field                     | H&H Construction                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary category          | `Remodeler`                                                                     |
| Additional categories     | **None**                                                                        |
| Rating                    | 5.0 from 10 reviews, all five stars                                             |
| Photos                    | 35                                                                              |
| Services listed           | 29, including _Kitchen remodeling_                                              |
| What reviewers talk about | communication (4), quality of work (3), reliability (3), kitchen remodeling (2) |
| Hours                     | Mon–Fri, 9 to 5                                                                 |

Hold the second row and the fifth row side by side. H&H sells kitchens: they're in the services list and the description, and customers mention kitchen remodeling in their reviews. The one place it doesn't say "kitchen" is the business category, which says `Remodeler`, the general parent. Categories are one of the main ways Google matches a profile to a search, so that gap costs more than any of the others.

This is the most common gap you'll find on a trade profile, and the cheapest to close. Owners tend to pick one category at setup, the broadest one that feels true, and don't revisit it. Whoever has a free afternoon fills in the services list later, and checking that the two agree isn't anyone's job.

```text
Use OpenSEO to pull the Google Business Profile for [business name] in
[town, state]. List every service I sell, from my services list, my
description and what my reviews talk about. For each one, tell me whether
my primary or additional categories cover it. Flag any service I sell
that no category names, and suggest the closest Google category for it.
```

## Local rank tracking: check your Google Maps ranking where customers search

If you search for your own service from your office, you'll look great, because you're standing on top of your own pin and your customers aren't. So we checked _kitchen remodeling_ from nine spots around Cookeville, about five miles apart, the way a phone would see it from each one:

![A 3 by 3 grid of Google Maps positions for H&H Construction on the search kitchen remodeling around Cookeville, Tennessee, mobile, 8 km between points, pulled September 24, 2026. North row: 2, 3, 5. Middle row: 1, 1, 3. South row: 1, 1, 2. Average position 2.11, top three at 8 of 9 points. Every point H&H does not win is won by Cookeville Kitchen Sales.](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses/local-rank-grid-kitchen-remodeling-openseo.png)

At the front door, first. Due west and due south, first. Drive north-east and H&H slides to third, then fifth. A homeowner in the north end of town searching for a kitchen remodeler sees four other companies before this one, and the same business takes all five points H&H doesn't win.

A single rank check would have told you "you're #1" and you'd have moved on. This is local rank tracking: your Google Maps ranking from nine places instead of one, and it shows you where the jobs are leaking. Each point is one map search, so a 3×3 grid is nine searches and a 5×5 is 25, and each one uses credits. Start at 3×3. The [local rank tracking library](/library/rank-tracking/local-rank-tracking) covers how to size and space a grid for your area.

### Check your Google Maps ranking with your phone

Search your main service from three or four different parts of your service area, on your phone, with location on, somewhere other than the shop. Write down where you come up at each stop. If the number gets worse as you drive in one direction, look up who's winning there. That's the business to study next.

### Local SEO for service area businesses

Plenty of plumbers, cleaners and landscapers run what Google calls a service area business: no storefront, and the address hidden on the profile. The check works the same way: centre the grid on your address anyway, then add a check or two from the towns you most want work from. Those are the points that tell you whether your profile reaches the jobs you're driving to.

```text
Use OpenSEO to run a 3x3 local rank grid for "[main service]" centred on
my business at [address], 8 km between points, on mobile. Show my position
at each point. For every point where I'm not #1, name the business that is,
and tell me whether it's closer to that point than I am.
```

## Local competitor analysis: compare your profile to the business outranking you

Compare against the business taking the points you lost, even if it's smaller than the big names in town. (The [competitor analysis skill](/docs/skills/competitor-analysis) does this for websites; for map listings, the profile pull below is enough.) For H&H, that's Cookeville Kitchen Sales, a kitchen showroom 3.9 km north-north-east of H&H's office. The two profiles compare like this:

![Side-by-side comparison of two Google Business Profiles, both re-checked September 28, 2026. Category: H&H Construction, Remodeler; Cookeville Kitchen Sales, Kitchen remodeler. Rating 5.0 against 4.6. Reviews 10 against 67. Photos 35 against 67. Services listed 29 against 11. Attributes set 4 against 8. H&H wins rating and services; the competitor wins category, reviews, photos and attributes.](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses/gbp-head-to-head-openseo.png)

H&H wins on rating and on range of services. It loses on an exact-match category and a review count 6.7 times smaller, which map to two of Google's three factors: relevance and prominence.

Before you blame distance, test it. We measured each grid point's distance to both businesses:

| Grid point                      | To H&H    | To the competitor | Who's #1       |
| ------------------------------- | --------- | ----------------- | -------------- |
| North-west                      | 11.3 km   | 11.3 km           | **Competitor** |
| North                           | 8.0 km    | 5.3 km            | Competitor     |
| North-east                      | 11.3 km   | 7.6 km            | Competitor     |
| East                            | 8.0 km    | 6.6 km            | Competitor     |
| West, centre, south-west, south | 0–11.3 km | 3.9–15.2 km       | H&H            |
| South-east                      | 11.3 km   | 12.6 km           | **Competitor** |

Distance explains most of the map. Wherever the showroom is closer, it wins, and no setting will change where it's parked. But look at the two rows in bold. In the north-west the two businesses are the same distance away, and in the south-east H&H is 1.3 km _closer_. The showroom wins both. On a tie, the business whose category says "kitchen" and whose review count is 67 came out ahead. Those are the two rows you can change.

```text
Use OpenSEO to pull the Google Business Profile for [competitor name] in
[town]. Put it next to mine in one table: primary category, additional
categories, rating, review count, photos, services and attributes. Mark
which of us wins each row. Then tell me which rows I can change this week
and which will take months.
```

## How to get more Google reviews: ask on the day the job ends

You can fix a category in five minutes. You can't close a 10-to-67 gap in Google reviews in a month. It's still the second job, because it's where the showroom is furthest ahead. [Drew Dorenfest](https://unscriptedseo.com/drew-dorenfest-seo-for-small-businesses-cited-content-the-trailer-editor-mindset/), who runs SEO and review systems for small businesses, explained why it matters so much in a close race:

![A quote card reading: Whoever has the most reviews, the most recent reviews is often gonna be near the top, especially if you're apples to apples. Attributed to Drew Dorenfest, founder of Client Magnet CRM, from The Unscripted SEO Interview Podcast.](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses/quote-most-recent-reviews.png)

"Apples to apples" is the north-west corner of that grid: same distance, same service, and the tie goes to the pile of recent reviews. Drew's own fix is boring and it works: an automatic thank-you email with a direct link to leave a Google review, sent when a job is marked complete, so the ask doesn't depend on anyone's memory. Reviews are the local version of links, and if you want the website side too, the [link prospecting skill](/docs/skills/link-prospecting) finds local sites worth a mention and the free [backlink checker](/backlink-checker) shows who links to the business beating you.

The better moment is before the email, while you're still standing in the finished kitchen. Josie Davis, who co-owns H&H with her husband Alec, [described what their customers say at the end of a job](https://unscriptedsmallbusiness.com/dominating-cookeville-home-renovation-additions-remodeling-hh-construction/): "we don't know what we're gonna do once we're not talking to you every day." That's the sentence to listen for. A customer who says it will write you a review if you ask.

![A quote card reading: Construction is a marriage of certain form. You're gonna be spending a lot of time with those people. Attributed to Josie Davis, co-owner of H&H Construction, from The Unscripted Small Business Podcast.](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses/quote-marriage-of-certain-form.png)

Her ten reviews are all five stars, and that's worth protecting while you grow the count. Ask the customers who would describe the job the way she describes the work. Don't buy reviews, and don't offer anything for them.

## Your local SEO checklist for this week

### The fix: add `Kitchen remodeler` as an additional category

It's free and it takes about five minutes in the profile editor. The primary category stays `Remodeler`, so the general remodeling searches aren't touched. The business already does the work, and customers already say so in reviews. The category is the only place that doesn't.

Set your expectations by the distance table. The two tie-break points, north-west and south-east, are where a category change has the best chance of showing up. The north-east corner, where the showroom is 3.7 km closer, should stay theirs. The goal is to stop losing the points where you're as close as they are.

A snapshot isn't an experiment, so don't treat any change in the first week as proof. Change one thing, wait three weeks, and re-run the exact same grid.

1. Add the missing category today, and change nothing else on the profile so you'll know what moved it.
2. Through the month, ask each finished customer for a review in person on the day the job ends.
3. In three weeks, re-run the grid with the same keyword, centre and spacing, and compare it point by point.

```text
Use OpenSEO to re-run my local rank grid for "[main service]": same centre
([address]), same 3x3 size, same 8 km spacing, mobile. Compare it point by
point with my last run from [date]. Tell me which points moved, which
business holds #1 at each point now, and whether my profile changed in
between.
```

## Local SEO reporting for agencies and in-house teams

Agencies and in-house marketers doing local SEO for clients get this job with an extra step: somebody else has to agree before anything changes. The grid is the thing that gets them to agree. An owner who has shrugged at ranking reports for years will believe a map of their own town with a competitor's name on the squares they lost.

Keep the local SEO report on what the owner can feel. Drew said it better than any reporting template I've seen:

![A quote card reading: Conversions are the only metric I care about. Traffic is nice, but conversions, more sales is something that the business owner can feel. Attributed to Drew Dorenfest, founder of Client Magnet CRM, from The Unscripted SEO Interview Podcast.](/blog/local-seo-for-service-businesses/quote-conversions-only-metric.png)

Lead with the map, name the one fix, and end on the date you'll check it again. The proof is calls and quote requests from the north end of town; the grid shows where to look. The [SEO report skill](/docs/skills/seo-report) turns the whole run into a client-ready report, and the [competitive landscape skill](/docs/skills/competitive-landscape) maps the whole pack, including businesses that aren't beating you yet. If you're new to steering an agent through this, [How to Prompt Claude Code for SEO](/blogs/how-to-prompt-claude-code-for-seo) is the companion to the prompts on this page.

```text
Use OpenSEO to build a one-page local SEO report for [client], a [trade]
in [town]. Include: their profile audit against the services they sell,
a 3x3 rank grid for their top two services, a side-by-side with whoever
wins the most points they lose, and one recommended fix. Write it for the
owner, not for an SEO: plain language, no jargon, and end with the date
we'll re-check.
```

## Why your business isn't showing on Google Maps across town

If you show up at your front door and disappear a few miles out, check Google's three questions in this order: distance, which you can't change; your business category, which takes five minutes to fix; and your Google reviews, which take months. For H&H, the gap was a category that didn't say "kitchen" and a review count 6.7 times smaller.
