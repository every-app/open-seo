# Cleaning copy

Most bad copy isn't badly written. It's copy that shouldn't be on the page. Editing it for tone just makes the wrong thing read better. So the first question is never "how should this be phrased" — it's **should this exist**.

## The ladder

Walk a string down these in order. Stop at the first one that fires and delete.

**1. Does this need to surface at all?**
Is the thing being described something the reader would ever have wondered about, unprompted? If they'd never have thought to ask, you're not answering a question — you're planting one. Nobody reading a plan for their own site wonders whether a move is realistic. A `why` reading "a realistic target for a site of this size" makes them wonder which of the other moves isn't.

**2. Does the label need to exist?**
A caption earns its place only when the value is meaningless without it. If the label, the field, or the value beside it already says the thing, the caption is noise. A move's card prints its searches and its difficulty beside it, so a `why` that opens "At 480 searches a month and a difficulty of 20" is repeating the card. Printed after "On page one now:", `holders` does not need to start "Page one currently shows". A bare "480" in a sentence does need "searches" beside it — that one stays.

**3. Is it pre-empting something the reader would never have had to consider?**
The tell: copy that rules out a failure rather than describing a behaviour.

> Add a table of the three plans and what each costs a month.
> _This does not mean redesigning the page or changing your prices._

That second sentence exists because the writer pictured an objection. The reader never raised it, and now has a worry they arrived without. Same for copy defending a choice, or hedging about something that simply holds: "results are not guaranteed", "this may take time to show", "volumes can vary from month to month".

**4. Rewrite to remove the offending part. If nothing is left, delete the element.**
Don't preserve a sentence just because there was a sentence there. A bullet with nothing left comes out of its list; a section with no bullets left loses its heading; a third step that only repeats the second is deleted, not shortened to a stub.

## What survives

Copy stays when the reader genuinely cannot act without it:

- A real consequence at the moment of choosing. In a move's `why`: _"The largest search in the plan, and the hardest page one to reach."_ They need it before they pick the move, and they can't infer it.
- A compatibility or scope fact they can't see. _"Page one is forum threads and app roundups, with no guide among them."_ The keyword alone doesn't tell them whether a guide has room.
- What a blank state means and what to do next. _"No page for this yet. Publish it at /pricing."_
- Units, scale, or format on a bare value.

Note the first example is one sentence. Had it continued _"so only start it once the first two pages are live"_, that half would go: it instructs the reader on when they're allowed to make a choice they can already see. Keep the consequence, cut the permission slip.

## Working style

- Read the string next to what's actually on the page around it — the label, the number, the line above. Redundancy is invisible when you read the string alone.
- Cut whole clauses, not words. Tightening "Add a short worked example that clearly shows" into "Add a worked example showing" is a smaller win than noticing which half of a two-sentence bullet is doing no work.
- Don't rewrite copy that passes.
